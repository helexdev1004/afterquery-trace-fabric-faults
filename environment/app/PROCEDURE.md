# Cutting room procedure

This is the whole of how cloth comes into the cutting room and is cut. Every
figure and every rule the room works to is here.

The mill has five inspectors and they do not all fill the report in alike.
Where an entry cannot be right against this procedure it is put right before it
is used.

## Rolls

A roll is recorded by its number, its length and its width, in metres, and by
the pieces the mill joined it from, their lengths in order from the head of the
roll. Cloth is
always handled face up, at the mill and in this room, and its left-hand
selvedge is the one on the left looking along the cloth the way the distance
along it is being counted.

## The mill's inspection report

Every roll is run over an inspection frame at the mill, and every fault the
inspector sees is booked in the order he comes to it: what kind it is, how far
along the roll it lies, how far across, and how big it is. Along is
metres from the head of the roll, the end that comes over the frame first, and
across is
metres from the left-hand selvedge. Both are booked to the centimetre, to the
middle of the fault.

## Laying up

A roll is laid up on a cutting table from the table's zero end. The first few
centimetres are squared off and thrown away, and **2 cm is trimmed off each
selvedge**, so no panel comes nearer a selvedge than that.

## Cut plans

A cut plan gives every panel cut from a roll as a rectangle of the lay: from
and to along the lay, in metres from the table's zero end, and from and to
across it, in metres from the left-hand selvedge. Panels are numbered through the roll in the order they are cut,
`R07-P012` being the twelfth panel of roll 7. Cloth between panels is waste.

## A fault on a cut line

A fault whose middle lies exactly on a cut line goes to the panel beyond the
line: the one further along the lay, or the one further across it.

## Measures

The room works in metres. A yard is 0.9144 m and an inch is 25.4 mm.
